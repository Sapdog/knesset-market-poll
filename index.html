    How the numbers are calculated

    Prices. Each Polymarket seat bracket uses its displayed YES price (outcomePrices), falling back to the last trade. Kalshi uses the bid/ask midpoint, falling back to the last trade.

    Seat ranges. A party's bracket prices are divided by their sum so they add to 100%. Each bracket counts at its midpoint (20–24 counts as 22). An open tail such as "<20" or "30+" counts as one more bracket of the neighbouring width. "Under 4" and anything below the threshold counts as 0 seats.

    Vote share. Where Polymarket has a vote-share market for a party, each share bracket becomes seats as share ÷ (100% − expected wasted vote) × 120, and a bracket under the 3.25% threshold counts as 0 seats. The wasted vote comes from Polymarket's below-threshold market when there is one, otherwise 5%.

    P(pass). The chance a list crosses the threshold: Polymarket's "Which parties will win a seat" market where it lists the party, otherwise the seat-range brackets at 4+ seats, otherwise the vote-share brackets at 3.25%+. Hover a value in the table to see which one was used.

    Kalshi. Kalshi's seat-count markets ask "more than N seats?". The gaps between thresholds become brackets, and the same expected-seats sum is applied. Kalshi refuses requests made from a browser page, so its numbers only appear when the page runs from knesset-poll-server.py or through a CORS proxy.

    Models. Expected seats: each party's seat-range expectation, scaled together to 120 and rounded by largest remainder; a party near the threshold can show 1–3 seats, which no real Knesset allows. Likely Knesset: parties under a 50% chance of passing the threshold get 0, the rest get their expected seats assuming they pass. Blend: seat-range and vote-share estimates averaged by each market's liquidity, then scaled.

    Coalition chance. Each simulated result draws every party's seats from its own brackets and rescales the total to 120. So when a small party passes the threshold, its seats come out of every other party in proportion to its size, and when it misses, its votes are wasted and everyone else grows. The markets don't say whose voters a party takes, so this assumes evenly from everyone.

    Simulation. 4,000 Knessets are drawn, each party's seats from its own bracket prices independently, then scaled to 120. Parties are not independent in reality (votes moving between two lists), so treat the bloc chances as rough.

    Live. Prices reload every minute. The trend uses Polymarket's price history for every bracket over the chosen window (6 hours to 7 days), reloaded every 5 minutes; its last point is always the live price, and the small change numbers compare it with the start of the window.

    Volume is lifetime USD traded and does not weight the seats. Depth: high ≥ $250K, medium ≥ $25K, low below that. These are betting prices, not an opinion poll.
